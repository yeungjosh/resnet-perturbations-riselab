Project Overview

This project investigates a fundamental question in deep learning: Do different optimization algorithms produce models with varying robustness to input noise?

🔑 Key Finding

Different optimizers produce models with significantly different robustness profiles under perturbation, suggesting that the optimization trajectory affects not just final accuracy but also the learned feature representations' stability.

4 Optimizers Tested
2 Datasets (MNIST & CIFAR-10)
2 Noise Types
9 Noise Levels Tested

Research Motivation

🏥 Real-World Applications

Insights into loss landscape geometry and generalization

Experimental Methodology

Step 1: Model Training

Train ResNet models on MNIST and CIFAR-10 using different optimization algorithms:

  • SGD: Stochastic Gradient Descent with momentum (lr=0.01, momentum=0.9)
  • Adam: Adaptive moment estimation (lr=0.001)
  • Adadelta: Adaptive learning rate method (lr=1.0)
  • Adahessian: Second-order optimizer using Hessian information

Training: 110 epochs with LR decay at epochs [30, 60, 90]

Step 2: Perturbation Application

Add two types of noise to test images at varying levels (λ = 0.0 to 0.25):

  • Gaussian White Noise: x' = x + ε·λ, where ε ~ N(0,1)
  • Salt & Pepper Noise: Randomly set λ% of pixels to 0 or 1

Step 3: Robustness Evaluation

For each (optimizer, noise type, noise level) combination:

  • Apply perturbation to test set
  • Evaluate model accuracy on perturbed images
  • Generate robustness curves showing accuracy vs. noise level
  • Compare optimizer performance under perturbation

💡 Why This Matters

The optimizer choice affects not just convergence speed and final accuracy, but also the geometry of the minimum found in the loss landscape. Flat minima (often found by SGD) tend to generalize better and be more robust to perturbations than sharp minima.

Understanding Perturbations

Gaussian Noise

Adds random values from a normal distribution. Simulates sensor noise, transmission errors.

x' = x + N(0, σ²)

Salt & Pepper

Randomly sets pixels to min (0) or max (1) values. Simulates dead pixels, bit errors.

x'[i] ∈ {0, 1} w.p. λ

🔗 Why Skip Connections?

Skip connections (shortcuts) allow gradients to flow directly through the network, solving the vanishing gradient problem. Each residual block learns F(x) such that the output is F(x) + x, making it easier to learn identity mappings when needed.

📊 Interpreting the Results

  • Flatter curves = More robust: Optimizer maintains accuracy despite noise
  • Steeper curves = Less robust: Performance degrades quickly with noise
  • SGD typically most robust: Finds flatter minima through inherent noise in mini-batches
  • Adam may be less robust: Fast convergence to potentially sharper minima

Key Metrics

Area Under Curve (AUC)

Overall robustness across all noise levels - higher is better

Critical Threshold

Noise level where accuracy drops below 50% - higher is better

Degradation Rate

Slope of accuracy decline - flatter is better

Optimizer Comparison

Understanding how different optimization algorithms work and their characteristics:

SGD (Stochastic Gradient Descent) Most Robust

Update Rule: θ_{t+1} = θ_t - η∇L(θ_t) + μ(θ_t - θ_{t-1})

Characteristics: Simple gradient descent with momentum. The stochasticity from mini-batches acts as implicit regularization, helping escape sharp minima.

Hyperparameters: Learning rate = 0.01, Momentum = 0.9

Pros: ✅ Finds flat minima, ✅ Good generalization, ✅ Robust to noise

Cons: ❌ Slower convergence, ❌ Requires LR tuning

Adam (Adaptive Moment Estimation) Fast Convergence

Update Rule: θ_t = θ_{t-1} - η·m̂_t/(√v̂_t + ε)

Characteristics: Combines momentum (first moment) and RMSprop (second moment). Adaptive learning rates per parameter.

Hyperparameters: Learning rate = 0.001, β₁ = 0.9, β₂ = 0.999

Pros: ✅ Fast convergence, ✅ Works well with default params, ✅ Handles sparse gradients

Cons: ❌ May find sharp minima, ❌ Sometimes worse generalization

Adadelta Medium Robust

Update Rule: θ_t = θ_{t-1} - (RMS[Δθ]_{t-1}/RMS[g]_t)·g_t

Characteristics: Extension of Adagrad that addresses its diminishing learning rates. No manual learning rate needed.

Hyperparameters: ρ = 0.95 (decay rate)

Pros: ✅ No manual LR needed, ✅ Adapts to problem, ✅ Robust to hyperparams

Cons: ❌ Can be slower than Adam, ❌ Less popular/tested

Adahessian Second-Order

Update Rule: Uses Hessian diagonal for adaptive preconditioning

Characteristics: Incorporates second-order (curvature) information through Hessian diagonal approximation. More informed updates.

Hyperparameters: Automatic adaptation based on curvature

Pros: ✅ Better convergence, ✅ Curvature information, ✅ Adaptive

Cons: ❌ Higher computational cost, ❌ Requires backward pass twice

🎯 Key Takeaway: Loss Landscape Geometry

The optimizer doesn't just determine how fast you reach a minimum, but which minimum you reach. Flat minima (wide valleys) are more robust to perturbations because small changes in parameters don't drastically change the loss. SGD's noise helps it explore and find these flat regions, while adaptive methods may converge quickly to sharper minima.
