What Are Perturbations?
Perturbations are small changes to input data that can significantly affect neural network predictions. Gaussian noise adds random values from a normal distribution, simulating sensor noise. Salt & Pepper noise randomly sets pixels to maximum or minimum values, mimicking transmission errors. Understanding how models handle these perturbations is crucial for real-world deployment.

Key Findings
Different optimizers produce models with significantly different robustness profiles. SGD with momentum often finds flatter minima, leading to better generalization and robustness. Adam converges faster but may find sharper minima that are more sensitive to perturbations. The choice of optimizer affects not just training speed and final accuracy, but also the model's resilience to real-world noise.

Training Pipeline

Data Preparation

  • MNIST: 28×28 → Resized to 224×224
  • CIFAR-10: 32×32×3 (RGB)
  • Normalization: (pixel - mean) / std
  • Batch size: 128 samples

Hyperparameters

  • Epochs: 110
  • Weight Decay: 5e-4 (L2)
  • LR Decay: 0.1× at [30, 60, 90]
  • Initialization: Kaiming Normal
Why ResNet?
ResNets solve the vanishing gradient problem through skip connections (residual connections). Instead of learning H(x), layers learn the residual F(x) = H(x) - x, making it easier to optimize very deep networks. This architecture enables training networks with 50, 101, or even 152 layers while maintaining stable gradient flow.

About This Research

Research Question

Do different optimization algorithms produce models with varying robustness to input noise? This project investigates how optimizer choice affects model resilience to perturbations, providing insights for deploying machine learning in noisy real-world environments.

Methodology

We train ResNet models on MNIST and CIFAR-10 using five different optimizers (SGD, Adam, Adadelta, Adahessian, Frank-Wolfe), then systematically test their performance when inputs are corrupted with Gaussian white noise and salt & pepper noise at varying intensity levels.

Why It Matters

Real-world ML systems encounter noisy inputs from sensor noise, transmission errors, adversarial attacks, and natural variations. Understanding which training methods produce inherently robust models can improve deployment reliability without additional robustness training.

Key Insights

  • Different optimizers find different local minima with varying robustness properties
  • SGD with momentum often produces more robust models due to flatter minima
  • Fast-converging adaptive methods may sacrifice robustness for training speed
  • Second-order methods show promise for balancing accuracy and robustness

Research Team

This research is conducted by the Mahoney Group at RISELab, UC Berkeley. Our work focuses on understanding optimization dynamics, generalization, and robustness in deep learning systems.

Datasets

MNIST: Handwritten digits (28×28 grayscale)

  • 60,000 training images
  • 10,000 test images
  • 10 classes (digits 0-9)

CIFAR-10: Natural images (32×32 RGB)

  • 50,000 training images
  • 10,000 test images
  • 10 classes (airplane, car, etc.)
